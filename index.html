What am I looking at?

Every structure in this scene is grown from one real tree record in one of NYC's decadal Street Tree Censuses (2005 or 2015 — pick one under Census year), fetched live from NYC Open Data. Species, trunk diameter, health, and location all come from the real record — nothing is invented except the branch shape itself, which is procedurally grown (seeded per tree, so the same record always grows the same shape).

Reading the map

  • Outlines are the official NYC borough boundaries — water is already excluded, so gaps between shapes are rivers/harbor, not missing data.
  • The blue underneath is a rendered water base, not photography — it's there so land reads as solid against the harbor and rivers.
  • The tan land beyond the boroughs — New Jersey (west), Westchester (north), and Long Island's Nassau/Suffolk counties (east) — is geographic context only, none of it has tree data of its own. Long Island's real coastline extends past the default view; pan or zoom out to see the North and South Forks.
  • Canopy color — Health: green = Good, ochre = Fair, red = Poor, grey = Unknown.
  • Canopy color — Species: each species gets a consistent hue; the legend below the map shows the top species in the current sample.
  • Legend swatches are toggles: click any entry in the legend to hide every tree in that category (trunk and canopy) from the map; click again to bring it back. A "reset filters" link appears once something is hidden.
  • Canopy color — Season: purely a display style, not real census data — each tree is recolored for Spring/Summer/Fall/Winter. Pick a season from the legend below; there's nothing to toggle here since it's not a per-tree category.
  • Census year switches the entire sample between the 2005 and 2015 censuses — same borough/filters, different decade. The two years have slightly different source schemas (2005 has no separate alive/dead flag, for instance), normalized to the same fields shown everywhere else in the app.
  • Watch it grow animates a crossfade from the 2005 census to 2015 instead of an instant swap — the old canopy fades out as the new one grows in. It's a real transition between two independently-sampled censuses, not a per-tree growth simulation: NYC's censuses don't share a consistent ID across years, so there's no way to know which 2015 tree (if any) corresponds to a given 2005 one.
  • Trunk size filters by real trunk diameter (Young/Semi-mature/Mature) — independent of and combinable with whatever's in the Canopy color legend above.
  • Branch diameter and depth scale with the tree's real trunk diameter (DBH) — bigger, older trees grow taller, more branched structures. Branch geometry only renders once you're zoomed in close; from farther out, canopy switches to a sparser, crisper dot set built specifically to stay legible at that distance rather than blur into a solid mass.
  • The scale bar and borough labels are derived from the real lat/long projection, not estimated.

Controls

  • Orbit: click/touch and drag.
  • Pan: right-click drag, shift+drag, or two-finger drag.
  • Zoom: scroll wheel or pinch.
  • Recenter: snaps the camera back to the current view preset, undoing any pan/orbit/zoom.
  • View — 3D / Plan: switch between an oblique angle and a true top-down map view.
  • Compass dial (top-right of the map): click to reset orientation to north-up without changing zoom or pan.
  • Tap a tree: any canopy point can be tapped/clicked to show that tree's real record — species, trunk diameter, health, borough, census year, and tree ID.
